Errors that matter most

At the 0.50 threshold the Tuned Random Forest missed 583 attacks (2.44% false negatives) and raised 798 false alarms (7.13% false positives). In security a missed attack costs more than a false alarm, so the low false-negative rate is the one to protect.

What drives the decisions

SHAP splits each prediction into a contribution from every feature: positive values push a flow toward drift, negative values toward normal. Averaging their size over 500 test flows shows which features the model leans on most.

Time-to-live dominates. Attack traffic in UNSW-NB15 tends to carry distinctive source TTLs (such as 254), so sttl, ct_state_ttl and dttl together carry more weight than all traffic-volume features.

Every scored flow on the Detect page gets its own SHAP breakdown.
